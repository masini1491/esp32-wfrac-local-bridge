## Pinned source reality and feasibility judgment

- [`lwip_close` in sockets.c](https://github.com/espressif/esp-lwip/blob/fd432e4ee2cfb7f7f1c7eb7227e0173412e7b84e/src/api/sockets.c#L817-L853): actual valid-socket `netconn_prepare_delete` non-`ERR_OK` returns `-1`; `ERR_OK` goes through `free_socket` and returns `0`. Merely calling close on an invalid descriptor or re-closing a successful descriptor and observing `EBADF` is **not** a native *valid-connected-socket* close failure.
- [`netconn_prepare_delete` in api_lib.c](https://github.com/espressif/esp-lwip/blob/fd432e4ee2cfb7f7f1c7eb7227e0173412e7b84e/src/api/api_lib.c#L185-L253): comment permits TCP PCBs to persist in later states. Internal `LWIP_TCP_CLOSE_TIMEOUT_MS_DEFAULT` (see [opt.h](https://github.com/espressif/esp-lwip/blob/fd432e4ee2cfb7f7f1c7eb7227e0173412e7b84e/src/include/lwip/opt.h#L2110-L2135)) is a *specific internal timeout* and not a guarantee for arbitrary caller-return.
- [`tcpip_send_msg_wait_sem` in tcpip.c](https://github.com/espressif/esp-lwip/blob/fd432e4ee2cfb7f7f1c7eb7227e0173412e7b84e/src/api/tcpip.c#L442-L464): non-core-locking send+wait path posts to mailbox, waits `sys_arch_sem_wait(sem,0)` without a finite total-attempt bound; this codepath is contingent on effective compile flags.
- [`tcp_close_shutdown_fin` in tcp.c](https://github.com/espressif/esp-lwip/blob/fd432e4ee2cfb7f7f1c7eb7227e0173412e7b84e/src/core/tcp.c#L425-L458): `ERR_MEM` can set `TF_CLOSEPEND` and return `ERR_OK`. One observed `close()==0` cannot confirm or deny this state, FIN transmit or PCB reclamation; do not access private PCB from unsupported thread/context.
- Existing root Linux probe [README](../../tests/espidf_linux_probe/README.md) / `sdkconfig.defaults` disables the internal loopback for older A6b/T1/T2A; never reconfigure that old fixture. Any R2-C2 candidate needs a **standalone** pinned Linux loopback fixture with independently validated effective `LWIP_HAVE_LOOPIF`, `LWIP_NETIF_LOOPBACK`, IPv4/TCP/socket/thread settings. R2-B's staged-only code is not published source and is not a permissible implicit build dependency.

## Minimum bounded natural scenarios (proposed, not observed)

1. **Baseline control**: valid in-stack connected TCP pair on `127.0.0.1`; no payload, no option change; close local client, then finish peer/listener cleanup only where actually safe and returned. Record caller-return elapsed.
2. **Peer-first normal close**: the peer safely initiates a normal close before client's close, without delaying the TCPIP core, keeping worker blocked or forcing EOF/error. Record exact actual ordering and return/error/errno. Do not assume FIN notification order.
3. **Small ordinary payload** (optional only if the fixture can prove finite completion): fixed short write/read, then normal close. No resource exhaustion, artificial queue saturation, transfer flood, linger override, timer tampering or deliberately broken peer.

Each scenario has a distinct execution/test run token, actual `close_enter`, returned `close_return_0` / `close_return_minus1_errno` **or** separately logged `NO_RETURN_OBSERVED_AT_WATCHDOG`, monotonic elapsed, unique descriptor identity/generation, matching test-owned callback post/return, last owner access, independent barrier ACK when callbacks exist, borrower/registration snapshot and conditional logical owner release. Do not infer physical free or SDK-wide quiescence. A finite externally enforced process watchdog records `TEST_TERMINATION` only; no async cleanup of an in-flight call, no pointer invalidation, no second close on an uncertain descriptor. Tests must preserve original partial raw trace even when a case times out or outer verifier fails; no evidence loss.

**Possible outcome classes**, all grounded in observed raw events: `NATIVE_CLOSE_RETURN_OK_OBSERVED` (the natural normal-path control), `NATIVE_VALID_CLOSE_ERROR_OBSERVED` (only a real valid-connected close `-1`/errno), `NATIVE_NO_RETURN_WITHIN_WINDOW` (only absence of return before watchdog, NOT proven SDK unboundedness), `ABNORMAL_NOT_OBSERVED` (all admitted trials natural-normal), `TEST_TERMINATION` (process killed externally), or `NOT_RUN / BLOCKED`. A slow-but-returning close is reported as a **sample latency**, not hard bounds. If a natural close-error does not appear, do not artificially induce one to obtain a PASS. Do not silently re-run until a desired abnormal event appears.
